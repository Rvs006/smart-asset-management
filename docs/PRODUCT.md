# Smart Asset Management Tool (SAM)

**One governed asset register per project — replacing the pile of per-trade Excel sheets.**
*Last updated: August 2026*

## Summary

SAM is a standalone web application for MSI commissioning teams delivering smart commercial buildings. Today those teams keep a project's asset data in seven-plus separate Excel "Smart Asset Registers", one per system owner, each with a slightly different column set — and the clashes between them (duplicate Instance Names, wrong references, drifting per-project rules) only surface at handover, when they are expensive to fix. SAM replaces those spreadsheets with a single governed database per project: you define the naming convention and reference lists once, import the project's own register template, and every trade populates against validated dropdowns with live cell-level error-flagging and project-wide duplicate detection — then export a clean, consolidated, handover-ready register whenever you need one. It is built in the same look, feel and stack as Electracom's already-deployed Smart Commissioning Tool, so it lands as one more part of a toolset the team already trusts.

## Customer

The customer is the MSI or M&E commissioning team on a live building project — specifically the commissioning/BIM information lead who is accountable to the client for one clean, complete, correctly-named asset register, and the trade/system-owner engineers (BMS, EMS, lighting, security, mechanical, fire, lifts) who fill in their own slice of it. They work on a laptop in a site cabin or shared office, cross-referencing spreadsheets and BIM exports against a handover deadline, while multiple contractors email updated tabs. Today they cope with a hand-maintained master Excel template, distributed and merged by hand, policed with colour-coding and hidden validation columns and a naming cheat-sheet that lives in someone's head. **Not for:** facilities/operations teams wanting a permanent live asset database, work orders, or BMS integration for the running building — that is the operational life of the asset and a separate, later product.

## Problem

The pain is consolidation and governance across trades. In the register owner's words, the nightmare is "duplicate Instance Names across trades, wrong levels and rooms, and QR and BIM columns that differ every job — none of it caught until handover." Excel cannot see across tabs, so cross-trade name collisions go undetected; free-text entry lets wrong levels, rooms, systems and BDNS equipment types through; and because every project genuinely needs a different column set (one job needs QR-required and QR-size fields, another needs BIM coordinates and cyber fields), a fixed master template never quite fits. The trigger is a handover review — their own or the client's — that turns up a duplicate or a BDNS/BIM name mismatch that must be traced and fixed under deadline. It happens on every project and repeatedly in the closing weeks, and it costs several engineer-days of manual reconciliation per project plus reputational risk and chargeable rework when a wrong register reaches the client's systems. They rate the Excel status quo about a 4 out of 10: fine until scale and multiple trades break it, and never reliable at catching cross-trade problems.

## Mechanism

SAM works by treating everything that varies between projects as configuration rather than code, through four engines already reverse-engineered from real registers. A **schema engine** imports any project's CONTRACTOR ASSET template so its columns, requirements and validation rules are data — the same application serves 171 Victoria Street's field set and Holborn Viaduct's very different one with no code change. A **naming engine** either auto-generates BDNS-aligned Instance Names from the asset's parameters (a segment builder: abbreviation, building, floor, zone, unique number) or accepts names created in BIM or entered by hand, checking each for uniqueness and format. **Controlled reference lists** (buildings, levels, spaces, systems, operational zones, equipment types) drive the dropdowns and the validation. And a **validation engine** continuously flags invalid references, missing mandatory fields, format errors and — the thing Excel cannot do — duplicate Instance Names across every trade in the project, each fault traceable to the exact asset and cell. In use, an engineer picks their trade, fills a validated grid where bad cells light up immediately, and the lead watches project-wide completeness and duplicate counts on an overview. It wins where fixed spreadsheets fail precisely because project-specific variation is where spreadsheets break.

## Why

SAM wins on fit and trust. It is tuned to the one job generic asset, CAFM and BIM-data tools treat as an afterthought — MSI commissioning handover — so cross-trade duplicate detection and importable per-project schemas are the core, not an add-on. It inherits the Smart Commissioning Tool's interface, stack and delivery model, so the team adopts it the way they adopted SCT: because it matches how they actually work and produces something defensible to hand the client. And it removes the only real objection a spreadsheet-native team has, through its guarantee: import your existing Smart Asset Register on day one, and if SAM can't reproduce it as a governed register and surface the duplicates and invalid references your Excel is hiding, keep using Excel — every register exports back to spreadsheet at any time, so adopting SAM is never a one-way door.

## Business model

SAM is an internal Electracom capability, funded from the same project delivery / commissioning tooling budget as the Smart Commissioning Tool; the internal sponsor is the commissioning/delivery lead, and the "purchase" is a decision to standardise SAM on a project rather than a per-seat licence. The value hurdle is concrete: SAM must save more than the roughly three-to-five engineer-days of manual consolidation and rework it removes per project. The natural first shape is a per-project pilot on the next building after 171 Victoria Street and Holborn Viaduct, measuring days saved. Anchors are the cost of the status quo (the engineer-days to beat), the Smart Commissioning Tool (the internal cost and UX benchmark), and generic asset/CAFM/BIM-data tools (the heavier external market SAM under-cuts on fit). Charging the client a data-governance service, or licensing SAM to other MSIs, is a later business-model question, not part of the Day 1 scope.

## Proof

The market is proven by what teams already do and pay for: every smart-building project already maintains a Smart Asset Register by hand, and generic asset and BIM-data tools are already bought — the demand for governed asset data is not in question, only the fit. SAM specifically is grounded in two real project registers that shaped its schema and prove the "every project is different" claim: 171 Victoria Street (structured naming, asset, network and smart-point fields) and Holborn Viaduct (adding BIM coordinates, lifecycle, documentation, cyber, QR-code and commissioning fields). It ships in the same UI language and stack as the deployed Smart Commissioning Tool v0.1.51, an internal tool the same teams already trust. First-party market signal is the next thing to gather: the mini-launch puts the offer and a mockup in front of the 171 Victoria Street / Holborn teams and the SCT testers, where the bar is one reply — and the next test after that is the Design relaunch, where the bar is one "can I try it on my project?".

## Goal

Near-term, we are climbing the signal ladder: the immediate rung is one reply to the mini-launch, then one "can I try it?" at the Design relaunch, then one activated user (a system owner populating a real trade register on a live project) at the beta invite, then first adoption on a project at the public launch. The product-level bar for the working slice being built now is the magic moment: import a real CONTRACTOR ASSET sheet and instantly see cross-trade duplicate Instance Names and invalid references that Excel was hiding. What would make us change course is the ladder's own rule — the same rung missed twice (for example, register owners see the tool and still won't run it on a project) is a signal to revisit the offer, the persona, or the scope rather than push harder.
